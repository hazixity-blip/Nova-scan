Problem statement

Everyone knows. No one can check.

Everyone knows

Press reports and FSSAI data already make food adulteration common knowledge in India — synthetic dyes in turmeric, melamine in milk. It isn't a secret.

No one can verify

A lab test costs ₹10,000–₹50,000 and takes days. There's no way to check in the moment of buying — so unsafe food keeps moving, especially through low-income communities.

The detection engine

Three steps, one traffic light.

01

Real-time spectral overlay

The camera view shows a live diffraction spectrum from a repurposed DVD grating. Align the sample and the reticle locks on, reading its chemical fingerprint instantly.

02

One-tap comparison

Scan a trusted "known pure" sample once and save it as a baseline. Every market sample after that is a single-tap comparison against it.

03

Traffic-light verdict

Green is pure, yellow is substandard, red is adulterated — a result anyone can read in the shop, with no chemistry background required.

What the app does after the scan

A verdict is the start, not the end.

Active whistleblower tools

A red result turns a shopper into a citizen investigator: a one-click FSSAI direct-link, an evidence vault that captures the spectral signature, GPS, and vendor ID automatically, and anonymous submission that never reveals identity to the vendor.

Medical & hazard intelligence

Every detected adulterant comes with a clinical brief — WHO classification, where it's typically found, and the long-term health impact — in plain language, not a lab report.

Community risk mapping

Scans build a crowdsourced heatmap of adulteration by neighbourhood, and local markets earn a "Purity Score" from aggregate resident scans — safety made visible, not just reported.
